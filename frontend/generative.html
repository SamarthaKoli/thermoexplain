What is Generative AI in Thermal Engineering? Foundational Concept

Generative AI learns patterns from examples and attempts to create or reconstruct similar data. In this lab, we test whether these methods can represent useful patterns in the available simulation fields.

info

Comparison for STEM Researchers
Architecture A Deterministic Bottleneck

compress Variational Autoencoder (VAE)

Main research question: “How well can the model compress and reconstruct a thermal field?”

Encodes 64×64 temperature field patches into a smooth lower-dimensional latent space (mean μ, variance σ) and decodes them back to reconstruct the original geometry. Ideal for pattern compression, field interpolation, and outlier/hotspot anomaly detection.

Loss: MSE + β·KL-Div Latent Dim: 32 Output: Smooth Isotherms
Architecture B Adversarial Game

casino Generative Adversarial Network (GAN)

Main research question: “Can the model generate synthetic samples that resemble the training data?”

A Generator network and Discriminator network compete against each other to produce realistic novel field patches from random Gaussian noise vectors. Ideal for rapid surrogate pattern sampling and super-resolution upscaling.

Loss: Minimax Adversarial Input: z ∼ N(0, I) Output: Stochastic Synthesis

What does this experiment teach us?

We can assess how well the compressed latent space preserves the thermal gradients. Notice that low reconstruction error alone does not prove all physical conservation laws (mass, momentum, energy) are strictly preserved across cell edges. The VAE acts as a smooth statistical compressor, which slightly diffuses extreme peak temperatures.

chevron_right Advanced Latent Space & Training Details (ELBO Loss Breakdown)
ELBO Objective Function

ℒVAE(θ, φ; x) = Eqφ(z|x)[log pθ(x|z)] − β · DKL(qφ(z|x) || p(z))

  • Reconstruction term: MSE loss across 4,096 scalar temperature nodes.
  • KL Regularization: Keeps latent distributions centered near μ=0, σ=1, enabling smooth interpolation.
  • β hyperparameter: Tuned to 0.002 to prevent over-smoothing of thin thermal boundary layers.

Are Generated Results Physically Meaningful?

Similarity to training data does not guarantee that a generated thermal field obeys the laws of fluid mechanics or thermodynamics. Use this scientific taxonomy to select the right tool for engineering decisions.

Tier 1 100% Deterministic

Real CFD Simulation

Fluent Finite Volume Method

Direct discretized solution of continuity, momentum (Navier-Stokes), and thermal energy equations across 2.4 million spatial cells.

  • Strictly conserves mass, momentum, energy
  • Exact viscous boundary layer resolution
  • Valid for design sign-off & safety sizing
Compute Cost: ~42 min / run (8-core CPU)
Tier 2 Latent Approximation

VAE Reconstruction

Dimensionality Bottleneck

Encodes genuine CFD slices into a 32-value latent vector and reconstructs them. Evaluates how concisely a thermal field pattern can be represented.

  • Preserves macro thermal geometry (>99% MSE)
  • May diffuse sharp wall-shear temperature gradients
  • Useful for anomaly detection & compression
Compute Cost: ~12 ms / patch (GPU)
Tier 3 Stochastic Distribution

Synthetic GAN Data

Generative Adversarial Sampling

Samples novel 2D field slices from random Gaussian noise vectors. Mimics visual texture and histogram distribution of battery cooling channels.

  • No intrinsic PDE enforcement (mass/energy unverified)
  • Can hallucinate unphysical hotspots or ripples
  • Ideal for pre-training surrogates & hypothesis seeding
Compute Cost: ~8 ms / sample (GPU)
rule
Mandatory Protocol for Engineering Use Before feeding synthetic fields into CAD or thermal management sizing, verify: (1) Inflow/outflow energy balance, (2) Wall heat flux equilibrium, (3) Boundary layer continuity.
