What is Explainable AI?

Explainable AI (XAI) helps us understand which thermal boundary conditions and flow parameters influenced a model's output. It makes high-dimensional CFD surrogate approximations inspectable rather than treating them as uninterpretable black boxes.

How should I interpret this?

The prediction is the model's estimate. A reference value allows us to compare that estimate with the available CFD simulation result. The difference helps us assess model error. Note: A surrogate model prediction is an approximation, not a physical guarantee.

1. PLAIN SUMMARY

The model predicted a peak cell temperature of 39.1 °C (within 0.3 °C of the CFD reference baseline), primarily driven by the low coolant velocity through the narrow 2mm cooling channels.

2. WHY THE INPUTS MATTERED

Both SHAP (+4.8 °C) and LIME (weight +0.48) agree that operating at Re=560 with an inlet velocity of 0.18 m/s develops a thick thermal boundary layer. As a result, the coolant cannot convectively remove the 35.0 W/cell heat flux rapidly enough before reaching the downstream downstream Cells (#7–#8).

3. RELIABILITY & PHYSICAL CAVEATS

The local surrogate fit (R² = 0.91) indicates strong localized reliability for this laminar regime. However, this prediction must not be extrapolated into transitional or turbulent flow (Re > 2100) without retraining on fine-mesh k-omega SST solvers.

Crucial Boundary Limitation

An explanation tells us how the model behaves; it does not prove that the model is unconditionally accurate. Always inspect cross-validation results alongside physical checks. This surrogate was trained exclusively on steady-state 10-cell pack simulations using a 50:50 water-glycol coolant fluid model.
